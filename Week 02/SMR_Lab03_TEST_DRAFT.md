## Decision and intended user

**Decision:** Whether to initiate a speculative equity position in NuScale Power
Corporation (NYSE: SMR).

**Intended user:** A long-horizon, high-risk-tolerance equity analyst who needs a
clear record of what would have to become true for the company to justify an
initiation.

**Valuation date:** December 31, 2025, to match the latest annual report used as
the baseline.  The market price is deliberately not inserted: it must be sourced
with its date and provider before calculating market capitalization or a return
comparison.

## Initial thesis

**Watch / defer; do not initiate a base-case position yet.** NuScale has an
NRC-approved 77 MWe reactor-module design and a commercialization partner, but it
remains at an early commercial stage. Its revenues are small relative to its cash
use, and the valuation depends on binding customer commitments, deployable project
economics, and the avoidance of further material dilution. This is a starting
hypothesis, not a price target or investment recommendation.

### Share convention

For the initial model, use the reported **2025 weighted-average Class A basic and
diluted shares of 163.732 million** only for historical EPS consistency. A
forward per-share valuation must instead define a current, fully diluted share
count at the valuation date and separately evaluate Class B / LLC units, options,
RSUs, and warrants. Do not mix the historical weighted average with an end-of-date
equity value.

## Consequential assumptions to test

1. The 77 MWe design approval can translate into bankable project development and
   binding module sales rather than only preliminary commercial interest.
2. NuScale can convert its ENTRA1 relationship into contracts with economic terms
   that produce positive long-run FCFF.
3. The company can fund the path to commercial scale without share issuance that
   overwhelms per-share value creation.
4. Cash and investments are available to shareholders after allowing for operating
   burn, restricted cash, contractual obligations, and required working capital.

## Unknowns / gaps

- The valuation-date share price and reconciled market capitalization.
- Fully diluted share count and the economic treatment of noncontrolling interests
  / Class B units.
- Project-level module pricing, margins, customer deposits, capital requirements,
  and timing of binding contracts.
- Whether contract milestones are sufficiently enforceable and profitable to make
  forecasts supportable.
- The appropriate WACC, terminal assumptions, and a credible normalized FCFF year.
- Treatment of lease liabilities, commitments, and other potential debt-like claims
  in the enterprise-to-equity bridge.

## Research plan

1. Reconcile the cash, investments, restricted cash, debt, lease liabilities,
   contractual commitments, and noncontrolling-interest claims to the same date.
2. Establish a dated, fully diluted share bridge with explicit treatment of Class B
   / LLC units, options, RSUs, and warrants.
3. Identify signed customer contracts and distinguish them from non-binding MOUs,
   framework agreements, and development discussions.
4. Build revenue, margin, reinvestment, and cash-burn scenarios based on milestone
   timing and independently sourced project economics.
5. Value operating assets with scenario FCFF, then bridge to common equity and
   compare the output with market capitalization and cash-adjusted valuation
   multiples.
