## Reviewed and adopted modeling decisions

On October 19, 2026, the participant stated that they reviewed and adopted the recommended modeling choices below. This records their stated adoption of the scenario policy; it does not change the origin of the analysis or independently verify factual gaps. The underlying facts and limitations remain.

| Decision | Recommended choice | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Investment action | Watch/defer; no initiation | Binding supplier economics and a credible financing path remain unverified. |
| Main case | Central delivery schedule, $200m/module, 35% pre-PMA margin | Use the middle scenario as a transparent reference; keep delayed and stronger cases visible rather than treating the middle as a probability estimate. |
| Delivery status | Treat every delivery as conditional | A modeled schedule is not a signed order. |
| Cost of capital | Show 13.03% CAPM alongside the conservative 15% reference | Make RF/ERP/beta explicit while retaining comparability to the original output. Beta 2.0 remains a scenario judgment. |
| Terminal method | Retain the 6× operating exit with zero/4×/8×/10× sensitivities | Avoid an unsupported low-growth perpetuity. The multiple is an assumption, not a peer-derived fact. |
| Growth perpetuity | Exclude from the investment conclusion | Low-growth cells conflict with the fee path, and high perpetual growth lacks economic support. |
| Contract fees | Apply the 5% escalation floor and full remaining tranches | Match the visible contract terms; retain 10% escalation as a stress. |
| Cash operating cost | Retain $240m annually with 2% growth | Preserve the explicit scenario cost base; require a 20% cost stress before considering initiation. This is a planning allowance, not company guidance. |
| Price growth | Retain 2% per year | Avoid assuming supplier prices automatically keep pace with the 5% fee escalation. |
| Service economics | Retain $5m existing service revenue, $2m per installed module, 35%/50% margins | Keep recurring service economics explicit and conditional; do not use them as evidence of contracted revenue. |
| Reinvestment | Retain 3% of revenue capex, $2m minimum, 10% incremental working capital | Require cash reinvestment rather than treating gross margin as distributable cash. These are simplified allowances pending project economics. |
| Depreciation and tax | Retain 20% of opening PPE and 25% cash tax on positive EBIT | Use a transparent economic schedule with no loss-year cash tax refund; no GAAP or tax opinion is implied. |
| Opening liquidity | Retain June liquid balances less $40m July–August allowance | Keep lag-period burn explicit; do not call the result a reported September cash balance. |
| Funding | Retain $50m cash floor, 70% of reference price, 3% issue fees | Stress dilution and fundraising costs; retain authorization and subscriber-value failures. Availability is conditional. |
| Shares | Use gross-award economic shares for DCF; show basic and gross variants for comps | Include exchangeable interests and awards transparently without mixing the EPS denominator into an end-date valuation. |
| Claims | Retain zero extra claims only as an explicitly conditional calculation | Do not interpret zero as proof that leases, tax agreements or contingent claims are absent; withhold initiation until the claim bridge is reconciled. |
| Peer use | Keep OKLO/BWXT separate; do not average implied values | Their stages and operating models make a pooled target misleading. |
| Driver bounds | Retain documented stress bounds and reject invalid inputs | Preserve the distinction between a contractual minimum and a judgmental price/margin endpoint. |
| Monitoring | Review filings and material announcements within five business days | Refresh the thesis when contract, liquidity or financing evidence changes. |
| Reversal rule | Require verified profitable orders, a fundable stressed plan and supported value 25% above price | Use a clear decision threshold; the 25% hurdle is a policy choice, not company guidance. |
